Interplanetary Frogger: Earth-to-Mars Orbital Relay & Cycler Sandbox

Conventional Mars missions rely on a single Hohmann Transfer, which forces spacecraft to wait on Earth for up to 26 months ($yyy$) until Mars reaches a $+44.3^\circ$ lead angle, followed by a 259-day coast ($xxx$). This interactive simulator explores an alternative infrastructure: populating the $0.524\text{ AU}$ interplanetary gap ($1.000\text{–}1.524\text{ AU}$) with concentric rings of rotating orbital stations or electromagnetic relay portals ("lilypads") so ships can launch in any calendar month and cross the gap in a fraction of the time ($y + x \ll yyy + xxx$).

How This Simulation Works: A 2nd-order multivariate space-time optimizer evaluates every launch day $y$ on Earth, every pad/portal index on each concentric ring, and every inter-ring transfer arc to find the fastest prograde ($+v_\theta$, counter-clockwise) route to Mars—even waiting longer on Earth when doing so catches a faster multi-ring "green wave."

Quick-Start: How to Use

  • 1. Set the Mission Date ($T = 0$): Choose a month in the 26-Month Calendar Picker or drag the Mars Initial Phase ($\Phi_0$) slider to place Mars anywhere in its orbit relative to Earth.
  • 2. Choose an Architecture: Compare Docked Modes (where the ship physically docks with a moving pad and rides it along the ring arc until the next transfer window opens) against the Non-Docking "Shooting Gallery" (Arch 05), where the ship flies continuously through 10 concentric Electromagnetic Relay Rings (`0d` dwell).
  • 3. Tune Rings & Randomize: Adjust individual ring radii, toggle ring spin trims, change Pads / Ring ($N$), or click 🎲 RANDOMIZE to test a new random orbital alignment. The dashed preview trajectory and telemetry ledger update live.
  • 4. Launch & Toggle 3D / 2D View: Click ISSUE MISSION ORDER ($T = 0$) to watch the ★ Lilypad Frogger vessel race the ● Hohmann Baseline vessel to Mars. Use the 🪐 3D Ecliptic / 🧭 2D Top-Down toggle above the canvas at any time (even mid-flight) to switch between the oblique 3D solar system perspective and flat top-down geometry.

What Variables Change What Outcomes

  • Mars Initial Phase ($\Phi_0$): Determines how far ahead of Earth Mars starts. For Hohmann, this dictates $0\text{–}779\text{ days}$ of ground wait ($yyy$). For Lilypad Frogger, close prograde phases ($\Phi_0 \approx +15^\circ\text{–}75^\circ$) yield rapid direct transits ($\sim 22\text{–}45\text{ days}$ in Shooting Gallery), while wide phases ($\Phi_0 \approx 150^\circ\text{–}330^\circ$) require longer prograde spirals or ring-riding arcs ($\sim 60\text{–}120+\text{ days}$).
  • Architecture & Ring Count ($1\text{–}10$ Rings): Switching to the 10-Ring Shooting Gallery ($1.05\text{–}1.48\text{ AU}$) provides 10 electromagnetic Lorentz kicks ($\Delta v_\theta \approx 0.33\text{–}0.70\text{ km/s}$ per ring) that cancel Coriolis and Keplerian velocity shear without docking. Docked modes use $1\text{–}3$ rings (or 24 intersecting cycler ellipses) to passively wait out phase gaps in orbit.
  • Pads / Ring ($N = 6\text{ to }20$): Controls angular spacing between pads ($360^\circ / N$, from $60^\circ$ down to $18^\circ$). Increasing $N$ shrinks the angular gap between portals, directly reducing both ground wait ($y$) and flight time ($x$).
  • Ring Radius ($\text{AU}$), Spin Trim & 🎲 RANDOMIZE: Sliding a ring's radius changes its Keplerian speed ($\omega \propto r^{-3/2}$); toggling Sub-Kepler (`0.64ω`) vs. Super-Kepler (`1.34ω`) alters synodic shear between rings; and 🎲 RANDOMIZE shuffles all ring starting phases at $T = 0$, prompting the optimizer to recalculate the best trade-off between ground wait $y$ and flight time $x$.

01 The Two Lilypad Paradigms: Docked Escalators vs. Non-Docking Relay Corridor

Why can't a ship normally fire straight across the $0.524\text{ AU}$ gap whenever Mars passes across our view? Because in the heliocentric frame, Earth orbits at $29.78\text{ km/s}$ while Mars orbits at $24.07\text{ km/s}$, and as a ship climbs radially outward, Coriolis acceleration ($-2\vec{\omega}\times\vec{v}$) and Keplerian shear deflect it. The sandbox models both rigorous physical solutions to this problem:

1. Docked Solution // Particle-Docking & Intersecting Elliptical Escalators (The Cycler Web)

A single Aldrin Cycler only aligns with Earth and Mars once every 26 months. By populating the interplanetary zone with concentric rings of rotating lilypad stations and/or a mesh of intersecting elliptical cycler orbits whose lines of apsides are rotated at $15^\circ$ intervals around the Sun, orbital intersections occur continuously.

True Particle-Docking Mechanics: The ship departs Earth strictly prograde ($+v_\theta$, CCW), targets a specific moving lilypad on Ring 1, physically docks with it ($\vec{r}_{\text{ship}} = \vec{r}_{\text{pad}, k}$), matches its velocity, and rides the rotating pad across the ring arc until the transfer window to the next ring's pad opens. Step by step, it rides and hops outward until it achieves a velocity-matched tangential intercept on Mars.

2. Non-Docking Solution // Architecture 05: "Shooting Gallery" 10-Ring Relay Corridor

To make straight-line sight-line shots physically real without stopping to dock, we place 10 concentric rings of Electromagnetic Relay Portals (Linear Induction Rings) across the gap at $1.05, 1.10, 1.15, 1.20, 1.25, 1.30, 1.34, 1.39, 1.44,$ and $1.48\text{ AU}$ (with an optional 6-ring preset also available for comparison).

Zero-Dwell Lorentz Relay Mechanics: A ship departing Earth threads the needle directly through an active physical Relay Portal dot on one ring after another (`dwell = 0d`). Each portal imparts a lateral electromagnetic induction kick ($\Delta v_\theta \approx 0.33\text{–}0.70\text{ km/s}$ across 10 rings) plus radial boost that continuously cancels the Coriolis and Keplerian velocity shear, allowing direct 22–45 day transits when Mars is in prograde view!
