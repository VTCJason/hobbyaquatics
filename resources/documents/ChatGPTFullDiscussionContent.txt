Do LLMs “get” metaphors?
What humans do

When you hear a metaphor, you:

map meaning across domains
feel resonance (“ah, that fits”)
use lived experience to judge whether it’s apt, clever, or off

There is understanding + judgment + feeling.

What an LLM does

An LLM does something narrower — and surprisingly effective:

It has seen millions of examples where:
“X is like Y”
“Think of A as B”
“Metaphorically speaking…”
It has learned the statistical structure of metaphor usage:
Which metaphors tend to explain which concepts
Which metaphors work for which audiences
Which metaphors fail and get corrected downstream

So when it produces or interprets a metaphor, it’s doing this:

“Given this concept, audience, and tone,
what metaphor has historically reduced confusion next?”

That’s not understanding in the human sense — but it looks like it.

The key distinction (this is the money line)

LLMs don’t know why a metaphor works.
They know that it works.

That’s enough to be useful.
It’s not enough to be conscious.

Why metaphors work so well with LLMs anyway

Here’s the subtle connection to embeddings:

Metaphors are semantic bridges
Embeddings cluster ideas, not just words
Good metaphors create overlap between distant conceptual clusters

So when you say:

“Context rot is like giving someone too many directions at once”

The model doesn’t imagine confusion —
but it has seen that mapping correlate with:

better explanations
fewer follow-up questions
higher downstream agreement

That’s optimization, not insight.

Do LLMs understand metaphors when you use them?

Practically? Yes.
Philosophically? No.

They:

track metaphorical framing
maintain consistency
extend the metaphor correctly
notice when it breaks

But they do not:

notice irony unless it’s explicit
feel cleverness
sense emotional resonance
care if it’s beautiful or shallow
